# Dexterity
Dexterity is often known as the god-stat of fifth edition.
If you max out this stat, you can be single-ability dependent.
Dexterity is a great stat for unarmored defense, dexterity saving throws, initiative, and for its ubiquitous skills and ability checks.
It also is a terrific alternative to Strength, as it can grant bonuses to attacks and damage.

From the perspective of the Mythril Forge, these bonuses to attacks take it one step too far.
Long has this DM sought a math-free nerf for Dexterity-based damage rolls.
Several variations were tried, none were simple enough to keep save for one… Take a look at these three vanilla rules as written first:

## Rules as Written
These rules have been **deleted**.

> ## Weapon Attack
> When you make an attack with a weapon, you roll a d20 and add your proficiency bonus (but only if you are proficient with the weapon) and the appropriate ability modifier.
> - For attacks with melee weapons, use your Strength modifier for attack and damage rolls.
> 	A weapon that has the finesse property, such as a rapier, can use your Dexterity modifier instead.
> - For attacks with ranged weapons, use your Dexterity modifier for attack and damage rolls.
> 	A melee weapon that has the thrown property, such as a handaxe, can use your Strength modifier instead.
>
> &mdash; Player's Handbook, page 14

> ## Finesse
> When making an attack with a finesse weapon, you use your choice of your Strength or Dexterity modifier for the attack and damage rolls.
> You must use the same modifier for both rolls.
>
> &mdash; Player's Handbook, page 146

> ## Attack Roll Modifiers
> When a character makes an attack roll, the two most common modifiers to the roll are an ability modifier and the character's proficiency bonus.
> When a monster makes an attack roll, it uses whatever modifier is provided in its stat block.
>
> ### Ability Modifier
> The ability modifier used for a melee weapon attack is Strength, and the ability modifier used for a ranged weapon attack is Dexterity.
> Weapons that have the finesse or thrown property break this rule.
>
> &mdash; Player's Handbook, page 194

## Mythril Forge Ruleset
The above rules are the root of Dexterity's problem.
The easiest way to nerf Dexterity without using any math is to simply remove those rules!
More specifically, any Dexterity-based damage bonuses are removed from the base rules.
Consider the homebrew variants of these:

> ## Weapon Attacks
> When you make an attack with a weapon, you roll a d20 and add your proficiency bonus (but only if you are proficient with the weapon) and the appropriate ability modifier.
> - For attacks with melee weapons, use your Strength modifier for attack and damage rolls.
> 	Thrown weapons are still considered melee weapons, so they use Strength.
> - For attacks with ranged weapons, use your Dexterity modifier for attack rolls, but not damage rolls.
> 	A melee weapon that has the finesse property, such as a rapier, can optionally use Dexterity in this way instead of using Strength.

> ## Finesse
> When making an attack with a finesse weapon, you use your choice of your Strength or Dexterity modifier for the attack roll, and for the damage roll if applicable.
> You must use the same modifier for both rolls.

> ## Feat: Exploitationist
> - **Prerequisite:** Dexterity 13 or higher
>
> You have a knack for finding weak points in an opponent's body and defenses.
> You add your Dexterity modifier to the damage of attacks using ranged weapons, and to the damage of any other Weapon Attack that uses the Dexterity modifier.
