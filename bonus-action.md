# The Bonus Action
## Rules as Written
In the original game, the bonus action was a rare but valuable addition to a player's turn.
However, it would often happen that a character would have no option to use this bonus action, making their character, in a sense, under-optimized
(because of how important the action-economy is).

The bonus action is also sort of wonky.
There's several edge-cases where you can or cannot use a bonus action for a particular mechanic, as written in the rules below.

> ## Your Turn: The Bonus Action
> Various class features, spells, and other abilities let you take an additional action on your turn called a bonus action.
> The *Cunning Action* feature, for example, allows a rogue to take a bonus action.
> You can take a bonus action only when a special ability, spell, or other feature of the game states that you can do something as a bonus action.
> You otherwise don't have a bonus action to take.
>
> You can take only one bonus action on your turn, so you must choose which bonus action to use when you have more than one available.
>
> You choose when to take a bonus action during your turn, unless the bonus action's timing is specified, and anything that deprives you of your ability to take actions also prevents you from taking a bonus action.
>
> &mdash; Player's Handbook, page 189

> ## Two-Weapon Fighting
> When you take the Attack action and attack with a light melee weapon that you're holding in one hand, you can use a bonus action to attack with a different light melee weapon that you're holding in the other hand.
> You don't add your ability modifier to the damage of the bonus attack, unless that modifier is negative.
>
> If either weapon has the thrown property, you can throw the weapon, instead of making a melee attack with it.
>
> &mdash; Player's Handbook, page 195

> ## Casting Time: The Bonus Action
> A spell cast with a bonus action is especially swift.
> You must use a bonus action on your turn to cast the spell, provided that you haven't already taken a bonus action this turn.
> You can't cast another spell during the same turn, except for a cantrip with a casting time of 1 action.
>
> &mdash; Player's Handbook, page 202

## Mythril Forge Ruleset
The primary focus of these changes was to empower characters in two ways.
One, by giving every character a chance to make an additional attack on their turn.
Two, by simplifying the rules and removing edge-cases.

> ## Your Turn: The Bonus Action
> Various class features, spells, and other abilities let you use an additional, special action on your turn called a bonus action.
> You have one bonus action to use on each of your turns.
> A bonus action is especially swift, being done concurrently or consecutively with your main action.
>
> The *Cunning Action* feature, for example, allows a rogue to use a bonus action.
> You can use a bonus action only when a special ability, spell, or other feature of the game explicitly states that you do something as a bonus action.
> You otherwise have nothing to expend your bonus action on.
>
> You can also use your full action to use a feature, cast a spell, or use some other game mechanic that normally requires a bonus action to use.
> This allows you to take multiple swift motions on your turn.

> ## Spellcasting: Casting Multiple Spells
> So long as you have the action economy and spell resources needed to cast spells, you can cast as many spells on your turn as you might want on your turn.

> ## Combat: Two-Weapon Fighting
> You can use a bonus action to make one weapon attack with a light weapon that you haven't yet attacked with on this turn.
> Once you do so, you can't make an attack using this weapon until the end of your turn.
