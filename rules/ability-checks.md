## Ability Checks
The Mythril Forge ruleset attempts to give greater flexibility and creativity with the application of skill proficiencies.
The DM must call for an ability check rather than a skill check.
If a particular skill seems applicable to the player, then they may call out a skill they are proficient in, and the DM can declare whether or not the skill fits for the check.
If it does, the player gets to add their proficiency bonus to the roll.

*There is no such thing as a skill check.*
The DM should not call for a particular skill for it reduces player agency in the game.
For more information about this ruling, read the Angry GM’s Philosophy about Ability Checks.

### Tools
This ruleset also grants a greater importance to the use of tools.
First, you strictly cannot succeed in using an artisanal tool if you lack its proficiency.
This extends to items like musical instruments and alchemical kits.

If you are using a tool that you are proficient in to complete a task, then you can add your proficiency bonus to your ability check.
If you also have proficiency in an applicable skill, then you can ask to receive that bonus as well.
In this way, you can double up on your proficiency bonus.
This rule is commonly invoked for picking locks, where a player can often receive a bonus from proficiency in both sleight of hand and thieves’ tools.
