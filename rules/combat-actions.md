## Actions in combat
### Attack
The most common action to take in combat is the Attack action, whether you are swinging a sword, firing an arrow from a bow, or brawling with your fists.

With this action, you make one melee or ranged attack.
See the "Making an Attack" section for the rules that govern attacks.

Certain features, such as the *Extra Attack* feature of the Fighter, allow you to make more than one attack with this action.

#### Special Attack: Disarm
Sometimes, you might want to grab an item out of an enemy's possession.
You can use the *attack action* to make a special melee attack to disarm a creature, knocking its item away.
If you're able to make multiple attacks with the *attack action*, this attack replaces one of them.
The target that you are disarming must be no more than one size larger than you, and it must be within your reach.

Choose one accessible item that is being worn or carried by your target.
You try to swipe that item away by making a Strength or Dexteriy check (your choice) against the target's Strength Save DC.
You have disadvantage on this check if the item is being actively held by the target.
You succeed automatically if the target is *incapacitated*.
If you succeed, you remove the item from your target's posession, which then drops to the ground.
You can choose to knock the chosen item up to five feet away from your target.

#### Special Attack: Feint
You can aid an ally in attacking a common enemy.
You can use the *attack action* to make a special melee attack to feint, distract,  or in some other way team up to make your ally's attack more effective.
If you're able to make multiple attacks with the *attack action*, this attack replaces one of them.
The target of your feint must be a creature of no more than one size larger than you, and it must be within your reach.

You try to psych out the target by making a Dexterity or Charisma check (your choice) contested by the target's Wisdom Save DC.
You fail automatically if the target is *incapacitated*.
If you succeed, one friendly creature of your choice has advantage on attack rolls against the target creature until the start of your next turn.

#### Special Attack: Grapple
When you want to grab a creature or wrestle with it, you can use the *attack action* to make a special melee attack: a grapple.
If you're able to make multiple attacks with the *attack action*, this attack replaces one of them.
The target of your grapple must be no more than one size larger than you, and it must be within your reach.

Using at least one free hand, you try to seize the target by making a Strength check contested by the target's Strength or Dexterity Save DC (the target chooses the ability to use).
You succeed automatically if the target is *incapacitated*.
If you succeed, you subject the target to the *grappled* condition.
The condition specifies the things that end it, and you can release the target whenever you like (no action required).

#### Special Attack: Shove
Using the *attack action*, you can make a special melee attack to shove a creature, pushing it away from your and knocking it *prone*.
If you're able to make multiple attacks with the *attack action*, this attack replaces one of them.

The target of your shove must be no more than one size larger than you, and it must be within your reach.
You make a Strength check contested by the target's Strength or Dexterity Save DC (the target chooses the ability to use).
You succeed automatically if the target is *incapacitated*.
If you succeed, you push the target 5 feet away from you and knock it *prone*.
You can opt to only knock the target over or push them away if you so desire.

### Dash
When you take the Dash action, you gain extra movement for the current turn. The increase equals your speed, after applying any modifiers. With a speed of 30 feet, for example, you can move up to 60 feet on your turn if you dash.

Any increase or decrease to your speed changes this additional movement by the same amount. If your speed of 30 feet is reduced to 15 feet, for instance, you can move up to 30 feet this turn if you dash.

### Disengage
If you take the Disengage action, your movement doesn't provoke opportunity attacks for the rest of the turn.

### Dodge
When you take the Dodge action, you focus entirely on avoiding attacks. Until the start of your next turn, any attack roll made against you has disadvantage if you can see the attacker, and you make Dexterity saving throws with advantage. You lose this benefit if you are *incapacitated* or if your speed drops to 0.

### Help
You can lend your aid to another creature in the completion of a task. When you take the Help action, the creature you aid gains advantage on the next ability check it makes to perform the task you are helping with, provided that it makes the check before the start of your next turn.

### Hide
When you take the Hide action, you make a Dexterity check in an attempt to hide, following the rules for hiding. If you succeed, you gain certain benefits, as described in the "Unseen Attackers and Targets" section in the Player's Handbook.

### Search
When you take the Search action, you devote your attention to finding something. Depending on the nature of your search, the DM might have you make a Wisdom or Intelligence check.

### Stabilize
The best way to save a creature with 0 hit points is to heal it. If healing is unavailable, the creature can at least be stabilized so that it isn't killed by a failed death saving throw.

You can use your action to administer first aid to an unconscious creature and attempt to stabilize it, which requires a successful DC 20 Wisdom or Intelligence check (your choice).
On a success, the target creature regains 1d4 hit points.

### Use an Object
When an object requires your action for its use, you take the Use an Object action.

### Improvising
Your character can do things not covered by the actions in this chapter, such as breaking down doors, intimidating enemies, sensing weaknesses in magical defenses, or calling for a parley with a foe. The only limits to the actions you can attempt are your imagination and your character's ability scores.

When you describe an action not detailed elsewhere in the rules, the DM tells you whether that action is possible and what kind of roll you need to make, if any, to determine success or failure.

## Bonus Actions in combat
- Item Interaction
- World Interaction
- Swift Attack

### Item Interaction
As a bonus action, you may draw, sheathe, issue, or stow one weapon or item.

### World Interaction
As a bonus action, you may interact with a variety of environmental factors around you.
You can pick up an item off of the ground, push open a door, or pull down a lever.
Many other actions fit within this category, as well.
A rule of thumb is that world or item interactions do not require an ability check to complete, whereas an improvising action might.
A rusted lever might require a Strength check.
At that point, it may take a full action to attempt.

### Swift Attack
As a bonus action, you may make one Weapon Attack with a light weapon that you are weilding.

## Free Actions in combat
### Brief Communication
You can speak up to ten brief words as a part of your turn in the heat of an encounter.

### Use Movement
In combat, characters and monsters are in constant motion, often using movement and position to gain the upper hand.

On your turn, you can move a distance up to your speed. You can use as much or as little of your speed as you like on your turn, following the rules here.

Your movement can include jumping, climbing, and swimming. These different modes of movement can be combined with walking, or they can constitute your entire move. However you're moving, you deduct the distance of each part of your move from your speed until it is used up or until you are done moving.

### Drop Prone
You can drop prone at any moment during your turn.

### Drop a Held Item
You can drop a held item at any moment during your turn.

### Withold Initiative
At the start of your turn, you can choose to withold your initiative.
This is useful in situations where you want to work co-dependantly with a teammate, or when you want to engage in mounted combat.

For example, imagine you are a halfling paladin mounted on a marvelous greyhound. In order to engage in mounted combat, both you and your mount must have the same initiative count.
Your greyhound rolled a 19, but unfortunately your paladin rolled just a 7.
You decide its best for your greyhound to await its master's orders to move.
